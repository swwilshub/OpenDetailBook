Thermally this looks almost as good as a closer, which is the trap. With nothing closing the cavity there is no vertical DPC to stop water tracking to the inner leaf (Part C), and the cavity is left open at the opening, which Part B usually requires to be closed.

What the numbers mean

ψ (psi) is the extra heat lost per metre of junction, over and above the plain wall and the window counted on their own. Part L (Approved Document L 2021, Volume 1) makes SAP 10.2 count every junction; the notional dwelling uses roughly 0.05 W/mK for a jamb, so a detail much worse than that has to be paid for with better fabric elsewhere.

fRsi is the coldest inside surface temperature as a fraction of the inside to outside difference. BRE IP1/06 sets 0.75 as the minimum for dwellings, which is the usual Part C test for surface condensation and mould.

The rules the detail follows

  • Keep the insulation line continuous: the frame should overlap the cavity closer, commonly specified as at least 30 mm. The sweep shows why: at 30 mm the reveal sits right on the 0.75 line.
  • Close the cavity with an insulated closer carrying a DPC, not a returned block. The old Accredited Construction Details (2007) drew it this way, and LABC and NHBC details still do.
  • Line the reveal and fill the frame gap with insulation or low-expansion foam, so no air path links the room or the outside to the cavity.
  • Get a BR 497 calculation for the real frame and closer products. SAP 10.2 no longer accepts the old ACD ψ values, and unassessed junctions fall back to much worse defaults.

How the calculation is done

A finite-volume solver on a 5 mm grid near the junction, following BR 497 conventions: 20 °C inside, 0 °C outside, Rsi 0.13 and Rse 0.04 m²K/W for heat flow, Rsi 0.25 for the fRsi run. The wall runs 1 m back from the reveal. The glazing is replaced by a 30 mm panel of λ 0.035, and the window's own heat loss comes from a separate run of the frame and panel alone with the faces that touch the wall made adiabatic, so ψ = L2D − Uwall×1 m − Lwindow.

Simplifications: this is the jamb only (heads and sills add lintel and sill bridges). The frame is a 60 × 70 mm block with an equivalent λ of 0.12, the closer an insulated block of λ 0.035, and the open void is held at outdoor temperature. Absolute values will differ from a product-specific calculation; the comparisons between details are the point. Regulation and guidance references are quoted from memory, so check them against the current Approved Documents, BR 497, BRE IP1/06 and your warranty provider's standards before relying on them.

Materials: brick λ 0.77, cavity insulation λ 0.032, plaster λ 0.57. Whole-house figures assume 24 m of jambs and 2,100 degree-days.

Replacement windows: what a good install looks like

A homeowner's walk-through of the Glass and Glazing Federation's installation guidance. Know what should happen before, during and after the fit, and recognise a bodge while the installer is still on site.

The job, step by step

What should happen at each stage, and the signs that it isn't. Page numbers refer to the GGF Good Practice Guide (Issue 3, 2011).

    Check it yourself

    Four quick checks using the guide's own numbers. Measure in millimetres with a tape; they won't replace a surveyor, but they'll tell you which questions to ask.

    Is this an escape window, and is it big enough?

    Bedrooms and other habitable rooms often rely on a window as the fire escape route. A replacement must not make escape harder. GGF p.41–45

    The room
    New window: the opening part when fully open

    How many fixings should there be?

    Frames should be screwed to the wall on every side where possible, with packers beside each fixing. Enter the frame size to see the pattern the guide recommends. GGF p.22–25

    Red dots show typical positions, looking from inside. Fixings shouldn't sit within 150 mm of a mullion or transom centre line (100 mm for aluminium).

    Does this pane need safety glass?

    Glass low down, and glass in or next to doors, is in a "critical location" and must be toughened or laminated, marked so the mark stays visible. GGF p.61–64

    Measure "floor" from the top of the floor covering. Next to a bath or shower, measure from the bottom of the bath or tray. On stairs, use the highest tread under the window. A window seat or a bunk bed under it effectively raises the floor.

    Is the opening square, and what size should the frame be?

    A surveyor should take three widths, three heights and both diagonals. The smallest width and height set the frame size, less a clearance for expansion. GGF p.15–16, p.18

    What's changed since 2011

    The GGF guide dates from summer 2011. Its installation advice is still sound, but several of the regulations it quotes have since been revised. Where the guide and current rules differ, current rules win; check the latest Approved Documents on gov.uk.

    Energy (Part L)
    The guide quotes a window U-value of 1.6 W/m²K or energy band C. The 2021 edition of Approved Document L (in force from June 2022) tightened replacement windows in England to 1.4 W/m²K or band B, and doors to 1.4 W/m²K.
    Ventilation (Part F)
    Revised in 2021 with stronger expectations on background ventilation when windows are replaced. The guide's line that you can simply opt out of trickle vents is out of date; ask the installer how the current rule applies to each room.
    Safety glass (Part N)
    Approved Document N was withdrawn in 2013 and its glazing rules moved into Part K. The critical locations described here carried over.
    Security
    BS 7950 has gone and PAS 24 remains the UK benchmark, now in a later edition than the 2012 one cited. Building regulations for existing homes still don't require it, but it's worth asking for on ground-floor and easily reached windows.
    Certification
    FENSA is one of several competent person schemes. Whichever the installer uses, you should receive a compliance certificate after the job, or the installer must notify building control instead. Keep it for when you sell.
